Prestige pricing: when a higher price sells more

Buyers use price as evidence of quality. What the research shows, a worked example, and when it fails.

Short answerPrestige pricing means deliberately setting a high price to signal quality, exclusivity or status. It works where quality is hard to judge before buying, and fails when shoppers can easily compare identical alternatives.

01What is prestige pricing?

Prestige pricing, also called premium pricing, sets a price well above the category average on purpose. The goal is not to cover higher costs; it's to tell buyers "this is the good one". It usually comes with round prices ($80 rather than $79.99), premium packaging and fewer discounts.

02The research: price changes the experience

Wine tastes better when it costs more. People rated the same wine as more pleasant when they thought it was more expensive, and activity in a brain region linked to pleasantness rose with the stated price.1

1Plassmann et al. (2008), Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. A $5 wine was also shown as $45.

Discounts can make products work worse. People who paid a discounted price for an energy drink solved fewer puzzles than people who paid full price for the same drink.2

2Shiv, Carmon & Ariely, Journal of Marketing Research.

For a store the takeaway is uncomfortable but useful: cutting the price of a product whose quality is hard to judge can lower how good customers think it is.

03Worked example: mid-market vs prestige

A skincare brand launches a serum that costs $8 to make.

gross profit = (price − cost) × units
$29: (29 − 8) × 400 = $8,400
$68: (68 − 8) × 220 = $13,200

04How to use it, step by step

  1. Check the category. Is quality hard to judge, and is your product hard to find cheaper elsewhere?
  2. Find the "too cheap" point with a Van Westendorp survey.
  3. Match every signal to the price: packaging, photography, copy, support.
  4. Use round prices. $68 reads as confident; $67.99 reads as a deal.
05 · Key takeaways
  • Prestige pricing sets a deliberately high price to signal quality, exclusivity or status.
  • A $68 serum earns 57% more gross profit than a $29 one in the worked example.
  • It works when quality is hard to judge; use round prices and discount rarely.

06Frequently asked questions

Does a higher price really make products seem better?
Research suggests it can, within a range and in categories where quality is hard to judge.
Should prestige brands run sales?
Rarely. Gifts with purchase or limited editions protect the price better than percentage-off sales.
