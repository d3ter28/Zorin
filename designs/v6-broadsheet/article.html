Prestige pricing: when a higher price sells more

Buyers use price as evidence of quality. What the research shows, a worked example, and the categories where it quietly fails.

By Dexter19 November 20265 min read

Prestige pricing means deliberately setting a high price to signal quality, exclusivity or status. For some products a higher price doesn't just survive, it makes the product more desirable, because buyers use price as evidence of quality. It works where quality is hard to judge before buying, and fails when shoppers can easily compare identical alternatives.

What is prestige pricing?

Prestige pricing, also called premium pricing, sets a price well above the category average on purpose. The goal is not to cover higher costs; it's to tell buyers "this is the good one". It usually comes with round prices ($80 rather than $79.99), premium packaging and fewer discounts.

The research: price changes the experience itself

Wine tastes better when it costs more. In a 2008 study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, people rated the same wine as more pleasant when they thought it was more expensive, and activity in a brain region linked to experienced pleasantness rose with the stated price.

Cutting the price of a product whose quality is hard to judge can lower how good customers think it is.The takeaway for a store

Discounts can make products work worse. In the Journal of Marketing Research, Shiv, Carmon and Ariely found that people who paid a discounted price for an energy drink solved fewer puzzles than people who paid the regular price for the same drink.

Worked example: mid-market vs prestige

A skincare brand launches a serum that costs $8 to make. It is weighing two positions:

Table 1 · Serum at two price positions
Mid-market at $29Prestige at $68
Profit per unit$21$60
Expected monthly units400220
Gross profit$8,400$13,200
Units needed to match the other629140

The prestige price sells 45% fewer units and earns 57% more gross profit. It only needs 140 sales a month to beat the mid-market plan.

How to use prestige pricing, step by step

  1. Check the category. Is quality hard to judge, and is your product hard to find cheaper elsewhere?
  2. Find the "too cheap" point. A Van Westendorp survey measures the price below which buyers doubt quality.
  3. Match every signal to the price. Packaging, photography, copy and support all need to look like the price.
  4. Use round prices. $68 reads as confident; $67.99 reads as a deal.
Key takeaways
  • Prestige pricing sets a deliberately high price to signal quality, exclusivity or status.
  • In the worked example, a $68 serum earns 57% more gross profit than a $29 one.
  • It works when quality is hard to judge; use round prices and avoid frequent discounts.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher price really make products seem better?

Research suggests it can. In a 2008 PNAS study, people rated the same wine as more pleasant when told it was more expensive.

Should prestige brands run sales?

Rarely. Frequent discounts weaken the quality signal. Gifts with purchase or limited editions protect the price better.
